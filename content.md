## Magic Kingdom, Sunday Nov 8 {#magic-kingdom}

Your 8am to 6pm plan matches the park's hours exactly. Resort guests can get in at 7:30am, and the park closes to day guests at 6pm for the first night of Mickey's Very Merry Christmas Party.

### Tickets {#tickets}

- Sami needs a child ticket (ages 3 to 9) and Dad needs an adult ticket (10 and up); Disney has no senior price. Aydin is free under 3.
- November 1-day Magic Kingdom tickets have been running about $165 to $200 per adult before 6.5% tax, and a child ticket is $5 less.
- Ways to pay less, best first:
    1. Ask Yusif's friend. Cast members can buy park tickets for guests at 10% off.
    2. Undercover Tourist or Get Away Today. Both are Disney-authorized sellers, usually a few dollars under Disney's price. Their prices include tax, so compare totals.
    3. Disney gift cards bought at 5% off with a Target Circle Card, then used to buy the tickets on disneyworld.com.
    4. AAA, if you're a member; discounts vary by region.
- Skip eBay, Craigslist and resold tickets. Disney tickets can't be transferred and are tied to a fingerprint once used.
- Link both tickets in My Disney Experience once you have them.

### Lightning Lane: what it is and what it costs {#lightning-lane}

Lightning Lane is a paid add-on that lets you skip the regular line by using a separate, much shorter entrance at a time you reserve. You don't have to decide now: Multi Pass can usually still be bought the morning of, if it hasn't sold out.

- **Multi Pass** covers most rides. You book 3 ride times, then add another each time you use one. It's priced per person per day; Magic Kingdom has been $39 to $45 a person in November, and the app shows the exact Nov 8 price.
- **Single Pass** is sold one ride at a time for Seven Dwarfs and TRON. Skip it and ride Seven Dwarfs at early entry instead.
- Aydin is under 3 and doesn't need one. Your parents can skip it too, since they won't ride much. When the three of you use a Lightning Lane, they can wait with Aydin or ride something close by.

| Option | Estimated cost before tax |
| --- | --- |
| Skip Lightning Lane | $0 |
| Multi Pass for you, Yusif and Sami | about $117 to $135 |
| Multi Pass for all five ticketed people | about $195 to $225 |

Rides for Sami, as a rough estimate: about 14 to 16 without Lightning Lane, and about 19 to 22 with Multi Pass. Early entry makes the morning quick either way. The difference shows up at midday, when Buzz Lightyear, Jungle Cruise, Big Thunder and Haunted Mansion tend to have long standby lines.

My take: Sami loves rides and re-rides, so Multi Pass for the three of you is the best value. If the app shows short waits that morning, skip it.

### Rider Switch: how it works {#rider-switch}

There's nothing to book in the app. You set it up at the ride when Aydin is too small for it.

1. Walk up to the ride entrance with the whole group and tell the cast member you'd like Rider Switch.
2. One group rides first through the regular line. The other adult waits with Aydin.
3. The cast member scans the tickets of the waiting group. Ask them to include Sami so he can ride again.
4. When the first group gets off, the waiting adult and Sami go to the return entrance the cast member points to and board without the regular wait. Up to two people can ride on the return.

## Eating well on the trip {#eating-well}

Your plan in one line: whole ingredients, cooked simply. No added sugar, no batter or breading, no cream or cheese sauces (cheese only if it's melted mozzarella), no shellfish and no alcohol. Butter, olive oil, whole grains, potatoes, rice, beans and fresh fruit are all in.

How to order: tell your server or cashier you're eating whole foods with no added sugar, no breading and no cream sauces, and ask for the protein plain with vegetables and a potato or rice. On the ship, have that conversation with your server the first night so they handle it for the rest of the cruise.

| Where | Best order | Ask for or skip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Riverside Mill, breakfast | Scrambled eggs, steel-cut oatmeal topped with fruit and nuts, fresh fruit | Ask whether the overnight oats are sweetened. Skip waffles, pancakes and parfaits. |
| Riverside Mill, lunch and dinner | Mojo-roasted chicken or citrus-basil salmon with two sides; Greek salad with chicken and no feta; a side of broccoli | No finishing sauce or glaze. Dressing on the side, or oil and lemon. |
| Boatwright's, Riverside | Char-grilled steak, plain, with vegetables; wedge salad without the blue cheese and candied bacon | Skip the fritters, panko chicken and shrimp dishes. |
| Magic Kingdom, quick service | Pecos Bill (on the route at lunch): rice bowl with black beans, grilled peppers and onions and salsa. Columbia Harbour House: grilled salmon with rice and green beans | Skip the shrimp skewer. Fruit carts are all over the park. |
| Magic Kingdom, sit-down | Liberty Tree Tavern: pot roast, mashed potatoes and seasonal vegetables | Ask whether the sauce is sweetened. Skip the stuffing and dessert. |
| Ship dining rooms | Lighter Notes: grilled sirloin, roasted chicken or salmon with garden vegetables and a baked potato or rice | Skip the bread basket and the lobster salad. Ask for a fruit plate instead of dessert. |
| Marceline Market, ship buffet | Omelet with vegetables (no cheese, or melted mozzarella), oatmeal, fruit, unglazed carved meats, salad bar with oil and vinegar, rice and potatoes | Skip the bakery, the seafood bar, juice, glazed meats and creamy salads. |

## Dad's cabin checklist: both staterooms, in order {#dads-cabin-checklist}

Dad, this one's yours. Those cruise videos you've been sending cover real things, and most of them come down to a few good habits. Disney ships rank near the top of the CDC's sanitation inspections, and the Wish scored 99 out of 100 on its latest one (86 is a passing score). So the plan is short: about 10 minutes per cabin on Monday afternoon, then a minute or two a day. You're in charge of both cabins. Do ours first, since the boys will want to explore.

1. **This week: pack a small cabin kit.** One zip bag for both cabins with one small pack of disinfecting wipes (we'll have baby wipes too), a small hand sanitizer, a flashlight (a phone light works), a few clips to keep the curtains closed in the morning, and a small plug-in night light. Leave power strips and extension cords at home. Disney bans them and takes them at security, the outlets sit right next to the beds, and Guest Services can lend a cord for the CPAP if you need one. Skip sheets and pillowcases, since bedding is changed between sailings.
2. **This week: request the CPAP water.** Fill out Disney Cruise Line's online Special Services form, choose Medical Equipment, then pick the option for a CPAP that needs distilled water. Disney usually delivers a gallon to the stateroom. If it isn't in the cabin by Monday evening, ask the stateroom host or Guest Services. Bottled water works for a night in a pinch.
3. **This week: put the must-haves in carry-ons.** Checked bags don't reach the cabins until Monday evening. The CPAP, every medication (including Mom's seasickness medicine), passports, swimsuits and a change of clothes all ride in carry-ons.
4. **Monday, first thing in each cabin: hold off on unpacking.** Before anyone unpacks or the boys jump on the bed, set the bags on the desk or in the shower, off the bed and carpet, and do steps 5 to 10.
5. **Check the beds.** With the flashlight, pull back the sheets at the corners and look along the mattress seams and the headboard for tiny dark spots or bugs. In our cabin, check the sofa bed and the pull-down bunk too. Finding anything is very unlikely. If you do, call Guest Services before unpacking and ask for another cabin.
6. **Wipe the spots everyone touches.** TV remote, light switches, door handles (hallway, bathroom and verandah), phone, thermostat, safe keypad, faucet handles and the toilet flush button. That's how germs get passed along, so these 5 minutes matter most.
7. **Test everything.** Toilet flushes (warn the boys it's loud), hot water and drains in both sinks and the shower, the air conditioning, the fridge drawer is cold, the outlets by the bed for the CPAP, the TV, and the hallway door locks. Set a safe code you'll remember and try it once.
8. **Count the life jackets.** They're on the closet shelves, one per person. Our cabin needs child sizes for Sami and Aydin. If anything's missing or the wrong size, tell the stateroom host.
9. **Check both verandah doors.** Our two cabins share a connecting balcony on Deck 7, and if the divider between them is closed, the stateroom host can open it. On the Wish the door lock sits above an adult's shoulder. With the divider open, anyone on the balcony can open either cabin's sliding door unless that high lock is on, so keep both doors locked whenever the boys are inside. Keep the chairs away from the railing, and never leave the boys out there alone.
10. **Report problems that same afternoon.** If something's broken, dirty or off, call Guest Services from the cabin phone or stop by the desk. Problems are easiest to fix on the first day, including moving cabins.
11. **Meet each stateroom host.** They usually stop by Monday, and they clean twice a day. Introduce yourselves and ask for extra pillows or blankets, confirm the CPAP's distilled water arrived, and pick an evening turndown time that works around the boys' bedtime, since that's when they make up the kids' beds.
12. **Safety drill, Monday afternoon before we sail.** Everyone goes, kids included. Your station letter is on your Key to the World card, your life jackets, and in green at the top of My Plans in the Navigator app, which also sends a reminder 15 minutes ahead. Life jackets stay in the cabin. Each cabin checks in with a crew member, so get you and Mom checked in and we'll do ours. Bathroom stop first.
13. **Before the ship sails: Mom's seasickness plan.** Mom takes whatever her doctor recommends before we pull away and puts on her Sea-Bands. If she needs more, Seacalm (meclizine) is available at Guest Services and the Health Center for ages 13 and up.
14. **Monday night: set up the CPAP.** Plug it into the outlet by the bed and fill it with the distilled water. If the water isn't there, ask the host or Guest Services.
15. **Every day: wash with soap and water before meals and after the bathroom.** The CDC says soap and water for stomach bugs, with sanitizer as a backup in between. The kids club has hand-washing stations at the door, and Sami uses them every time.
16. **Every night: a one-minute wipe.** Remote, door handles, phone and faucets in both cabins.
17. **If anyone gets a stomach bug: call the Health Center.** The CDC says to report symptoms to the ship's medical center and follow their advice. Keep that person in the cabin and away from the buffet, pools and kids club until the Health Center says it's OK.
18. **Thursday night: bags out, carry-ons packed, final sweep.** Tagged bags go outside the door in the evening, usually between 8 and 10pm (the Navigator app has the exact time). Passports, Key to the World cards, medications, the CPAP and Friday's clothes stay in carry-ons. Friday morning, check the safe, drawers, under the beds and the bathroom in both cabins before we leave.

## Around the ship: what reviewers complain about {#around-the-ship}

The Wish carries a lot of people, and most complaints in reviews are about crowds in the busiest spots. Here's what comes up most and how we'll get ahead of it.

- **The first day feels crowded and confusing.** The hallways near the Grand Hall and Marceline Market back up, and the layout takes a day to learn. Reviewers say it eases by day three. After the safety drill on Monday, take a short walk around the ship with the deck maps in the Navigator app.
- **Elevators are slow and packed before dinner and shows.** Only two of the eight elevators go above Deck 11. From Deck 7, dinner and the theater are a few flights down by stairs. When Dad or the stroller needs an elevator, leave 10 extra minutes.
- **AquaMouse lines can hit an hour on sea days.** Sami just makes the 42-inch minimum and rides with an adult. Lines are shortest on port days, first thing in the morning, at meal times and in the early evening. Head back from Castaway Cay a little early on Tuesday for a ride, since Thursday at sea will be the busiest.
- **The pools are small and busy, and the levels make it hard to watch kids and grab food at once.** Go first thing in the morning or on the island days, and have one adult stay in the water with the boys and another get the food. Aydin's swim diaper means the Toy Story Splash Zone only.
- **Shows and the small movie theaters fill up.** Get to the Walt Disney Theatre 20 to 30 minutes early. Disney Seas the Adventure is the shortest evening show at 45 minutes, which makes it a good first try for the boys.
- **Dining rooms are tight, and the shows can upstage the meal.** Tables sit close together, and at Arendelle the servers step away during the performances, so food can show up cold. If something arrives cold, ask for a new plate.
- **Breakfast at Marceline Market is a crush.** Claim a table first, then get food. At sit-down breakfast, ask your server for the Mickey churro waffles. They're free, but only on request.
- **The gift shop is small and doesn't restock.** Mickey's Mainsail gets busy around dinner, and items don't come back until the next sailing, so buy the Very Merrytime and birthday souvenirs you want early in the trip.
- **Lookout Cay means a long pier, heat and less shade.** Wear walking shoes, claim a shaded spot soon after you arrive, eat lunch before the noon rush, and bring sunscreen, hats and refillable water bottles.

## Ship rules: what not to pack {#ship-rules}

Disney Cruise Line screens every bag at the port and holds anything on its prohibited list, which can also delay your luggage.

- Power strips, extension cords, multi-plugs and surge protectors (a plain USB hub is fine)
- Irons and clothing steamers (the guest laundry rooms have irons)
- Heating pads and hot plates
- Any wine or beer has to be unopened and in a carry-on. Disney's site currently lists one bottle of wine or six beers per adult 21 and over, and no liquor.
- Snorkels stay out of the ship pools. Goggles and swim masks that cover the eyes and nose are fine in the pools; masks come off for the water slides.
